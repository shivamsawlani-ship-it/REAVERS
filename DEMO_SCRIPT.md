## The Safety-Honesty Defense: Why a False Positive Does Not Block a Real Brake

During judging and technical evaluations, this is the #1 question asked by safety engineers:
> **Judge Question**: *"If your inline gateway can drop CAN frames, what happens if your AI makes a mistake during a life-or-death emergency stop? If you block a real brake, people die."*

### The 3-Part Answer:
1. **The AI Never Blocks Actuators Alone (Asynchronous Separation)**:
   The AI runs in an asynchronous background thread over 64-message rolling windows (~1.0 ms) and only sets a threat confidence probability in a shared `AIContext`. The AI *never* runs inline on the per-frame critical path. Even if the AI outputs an anomaly score of 0.99 on benign emergency traffic, the per-frame decision level is capped at `LOW` (forward frame), generating at most an asynchronous dashboard alert.
2. **The Strict Multi-Condition Confidence Gate**:
   A CAN frame is only blocked (`CRITICAL`) if three independent conditions are simultaneously met:
   $$\text{Deterministic Severity } (\text{Rules / Physics}) \ge 0.70 \quad \text{AND} \quad \text{AI Confidence} \ge 0.60 \quad \text{AND} \quad \text{Criticality} \ge 0.80$$
   If a driver stomps on the brake pedal or the autonomous vehicle triggers emergency automatic braking, the physics engine observes:
   - Valid brake pedal pressure sensor signals ($> 0\%$), OR
   - Forward radar/LiDAR tracks confirming an imminent obstacle ($TTC < 1.8\text{ s}$).
   Because the physical invariant is satisfied, $\text{Deterministic Severity} = 0.0$. The frame bypasses blocking entirely and forwards instantly to the brake calipers.
3. **Fail-Open Default for Actuation**:
   In ambiguous cases where deterministic physics cannot prove physical impossibility, CAVShield defaults to forwarding the actuation command. It protects against false-positive intervention while relying on hard physical laws (conservation of energy, hydraulic pressure, sensor track consensus) to catch true physical attacks.
